# Hosted sample live evidence, 2026-11-05

Partial evidence for [#42](https://github.com/tcortega/Whatsmeow.NET/issues/42), captured through 20:30:26 UTC.
The seven-day full-sync comparison and the issue's receipt-to-handler latency measurement remain open; this is not the Phase 3 exit.

## Scope and method

[observed: build metadata] The sample and client informational version was `0.0.0-alpha.0.69+70f1cf34fcf1cf3d3c643644ba66358b96cb20d8`.
The local sidecar's `go version -m` reports that revision with `vcs.modified=false`, Go 1.27.3, `darwin/arm64`, `CGO_ENABLED=1`, and whatsmeow `56e5ad0581ce`.
This measures that local build, not a packaged release or a build with `CGO_ENABLED=0`.

[observed: coordinator record and `echobot.log`] The owner paired two accounts through the unmodified EchoBot, Alice at 19:54:08.998 UTC and Bob at 19:54:30.051 UTC, in a new sample database.
Alice was a personal account and Bob a Business account, so this was not the issue's dedicated-test-number setup.
The initial run was stopped before the controlled exchanges.

[observed: `driver-v1-source.cs`] The subsequent temporary driver declared the same two sessions through public `AddWhatsmeow`, `AddSession` and DI handler registration, using the sample's compiled `EchoHandler`, `StateLog` and `HistoryLog`.
It invoked `EchoHandler` only for the other sample account and an exact text authorized by the driver, filtering ambient messages.
It did not invoke `AddEchoBot` or pair either session; the controlled phase is adapted instrumentation, separate from the unmodified sample run.
Both phases used default history options [observed: `run-sample.py`, `run-driver.py`].

[observed: `driver-v1-source.cs`] Each probe starts a monotonic clock immediately before registering its waiters and calling `SendTextAsync`.
Send-to-handler ends on entry into the receiving session's measurement handler; send-to-echo ends on entry into the sender's measurement handler for the returned echo.
Both include local setup before the send, and neither measures sidecar receipt-to-handler latency.
The measurement handler runs before the sample's echo handler.

[observed: `driver.log`, `driver-results.log`] The first batch completed between 20:10:08.560 and 20:10:30.145 UTC, while Alice's history count rose from 184 to 268 batches.
Bob received the requests; Alice received the returned echoes during its history delivery.
The reverse batch completed between 20:11:03.936 and 20:11:25.528 UTC, after Alice's last history event at 20:11:02.033777 UTC, with the count fixed at 388.
It is not evidence of requests arriving at Alice while its history was pending.
The quiet exchanges finished at 20:21:57.068 UTC.

[observed: `echobot.log`] The unmodified sample also completed Alice echoes before later Alice history: 17 of its 19 echo-completion records precede the last history batch, including 12 between the first and last `Recent` batches.
[observed: `EchoHandler.HandleAsync`] The sample logs after awaiting the reply send, and the embedded timestamp is `MessageReceived.Timestamp`.
Its log-time minus message-time difference is timestamp-to-echo-completion, not handler-entry or receipt-to-handler latency.
[inferred] These observations show live traffic interleaved with history delivery; they do not isolate history as a latency cause, exercise a deliberately blocked history handler, or establish a performance threshold.

[observed: `driver-results.log`, final stats] All 20 controlled probes had one observed receive and one observed echo, both at their initial pass and at the final recheck: ten in each direction.
Both sessions ended `CaughtUp`.
[observed: coordinator process record] The driver then stopped with exit code 0.
[unknown] These counts do not establish exactly-once delivery outside this observation window.

[observed: `driver.log`, `driver-results.log`] Alice became `Disconnected` at 20:14:34.716 UTC, `Connecting` at 20:14:35.460, `Connected` at 20:14:37.323 and `CaughtUp` at 20:14:37.411.
During the disconnect, Bob sent to Alice's account, received `Delivered`, and passed a check that it remained connected or caught up.
No echo from the disconnected Alice handler was required.
[inferred] This supports Bob remaining usable during a local disconnect of Alice; it does not test phone-side logout or receipt by Alice's disconnected .NET handler.
[unknown] The observed `CaughtUp` states do not establish that every future login produces `OfflineSyncCompleted`.
